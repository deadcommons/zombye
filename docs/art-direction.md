# Art direction

How Zombye looks, so every asset (built in Blender, started in Meshy, or generated by the game's
code) belongs to the same world. [asset-pipeline.md](asset-pipeline.md) is the process and the
sizes; this is the look. Colours live in one place, [`assets/data/palette.ron`](../assets/data/palette.ron),
read by the Blender tools (`tools/blender/palette.py`) and the client alike; this doc says how to use them.

## The look in one sentence

**A small early-1990s American town after the end, in faceted low-poly, where you are alone and
something is always out there: a lived-in, decaying world of muted colour, shaped by light, warm and
hazy by day and truly dark at night, read from high above; frightening by what you can't see, never
by gore.**

The mood is **isolation and fear** (the owner, 2026-10-24). Everything on screen serves it: the town
was full of people a few weeks ago and isn't now; the light shows you a little and hides the rest.
Six rules follow:

1. **Light makes the mood.** A low warm sun with long hard shadows and haze by day; real darkness at
   night and in unlit rooms, where light comes only from sources (a flashlight, a candle, moonlight
   through a window) and pools of it are islands. What's beyond your light or your sight is dark, not
   just dimmer. Every frame is graded (see Light and mood), so the palette is the starting point,
   not what ends up on screen.
2. **Faceted, flat-shaded, hard edges.** Every face reads as a plane of one colour. No smooth
   shading on hard-surface things, no outlines or ink lines, no painted shading baked into colours.
3. **Lived-in, then left.** Nothing is pristine: cracked and heaved asphalt, weeds in the cracks,
   leaf litter, paper, rubble, broken fences, cars left where they stopped, doors ajar, things dropped
   in a hurry. Every frame should hold a few things that tell you what happened here (see Decay and
   clutter). Emptiness reads as a game not finished, not as loneliness.
4. **Few colours per asset, a muted world, sparing accents.** Three to six palette colours for a prop
   or a house, one dominant; variety from picking a different set per building or outfit. The town is
   desaturated and dusty; the brand colours (zombie green, red, bone white, near-black) are accents: a
   sign, a hydrant, a zombie's skin, the UI.
5. **Simple shapes, a few telling details.** The silhouette says what it is; then two or three
   details make it ours: window trim and a porch on a house, a handle and door line on a fridge,
   burners on a stove, a flag on a mailbox. Nothing that matters is smaller than about 5 cm (one
   pixel at default zoom is ~3.6 cm).
6. **Fear from what you can't see, not from gore.** Darkness, silence, a door left open, a shape at
   the edge of the light, a stooped figure that doesn't move. Blood is a few muted dark stains, never
   wounds, organs or bones.

## Colour

### The palette

`assets/data/palette.ron` is the only place colours are defined (Blender validation refuses any other,
and the client draws the generated world from it). The asset groups:

| Group | Entries | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Brand accents | `brand_zombie_green` #8DB06B, `brand_red` #D7262B, `brand_bone_white` #F2E6C9, `brand_near_black` #141414 | signs, hydrants, toys, stripes; zombie skin is toned from the green; UI |
| Wood | pine, oak, walnut, raw | furniture, floors, fences |
| Kitchen and bathroom | laminate almond, cabinet oak, enamel white and almond, porcelain, water | appliances, fittings |
| Metal and plastic | steel, chrome, iron black, plastic black/grey/beige/red/blue, screen, rubber, paint green, cardboard, paper | trims, handles, small props |
| Fabric | mauve, teal, rust, navy, beige, cream sheets, white pillows, blanket blue | upholstery, beds, clothing |

**Adding a colour:** only when no entry is close enough (within about 10% in each channel) and the
asset needs it to read; add it to `palette.ron` in its group with a comment saying what it's for. Keep
new colours in the same family: saturation under about 45%, nothing pure, nothing neon. Brand accents
stay four.

### The generated world

Walls, roofs, floors, ground and trees are made by the client's code (`meshgen.rs`, `world_render.rs`;
the outdoors in `meshgen/landscape.rs` and `meshgen/trees.rs`, the buildings' details in
`meshgen/building_kit.rs`) and follow the same rules. Their colours are named entries in the same
`palette.ron` (ground and roads, trees, buildings, interiors by room type, floors, grey-box furniture),
with named sets for what the world picks from (`siding`, `roof`, `brick`, `awning`, `lawn`, `leaf`, the
floors, ...). The client checks every name it needs when it starts (`meshgen/palette.rs`), and a
`--data` folder can bring its own `palette.ron`, so recolouring the town is a data change.

| Surface | Look |
|---|---|
| House siding | one muted colour per house from a small set: cream, almond, butter, dusty blue, sage, grey, tan, oxblood |
| Trim (window and door frames, corners, fascia) | off-white on most houses, dark on pale ones |
| Roofs | dark slate, brown, rust red, moss, slate blue; a shade darker on the far slope |
| Shops and offices | brick red and tan, flat roofs with a parapet, awnings in one muted colour and bone white |
| Interiors | a wall colour per room type (kitchen cream, bathroom pale blue, bedroom dusty rose, living warm beige); plank wood, carpet, checker tile |
| Ground | olive-grey lawns in broad patches, longer yellower grass at edges, verges and vacant lots, a darker forest floor with ferns, rocks, stumps and logs; grey asphalt with darker repair patches and wheel tracks; paint at real scale (10–12 cm faded yellow centre lines, off-white bays, crossings and stop lines); pale concrete sidewalks with slab joints and a kerb; concrete driveways; brown dirt |
| Trees | pines and spruces as tiers of drooping faceted skirts; broadleaves as a tapering trunk forking into a few branches, each ending in a faceted clump; slim pale birches; two or three greens per tree, brown trunks, a gentle lean and size per tree |

## Shape and detail

| Thing | Shapes | Telling details | Triangles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small prop, ground item | one or two primitives | one: a lid, a label band, a handle | ≤ 300 |
| Furniture | boxes and blocks with small bevels on the parts you see | 2–3: drawer and door lines, handles, cushions, legs | 400–1,000 |
| Street furniture (mailbox, hydrant, bin, lamp, sign) | posts and boxes, a prism or two | 1–2: a flag, caps, a hood | ≤ 500 |
| Vehicle | a boxy early-90s body in two or three blocks | wheels, bumpers, lights, windows as dark panels | ≤ 3,000 |
| Character | one faceted body, chunky hands and feet | face as a few planes and colours, collar, belt, shoes | ~3,000 |

- **Bevels** only where they catch the light from above (table tops, cushions, counter fronts), one
  segment, then apply. Small hard bevels are what make low-poly look made rather than unfinished.
- **Proportions** may be pushed a little for readability: handles a bit big, shapes a bit chunky,
  never cartoon-squashed.
- **Facing up matters most.** The camera sits 30° above the horizon, so tops (beds, tables, counters,
  car roofs) are what the player sees. Put the telling details on top and on the front.
- **Wear** is shape, not texture: a tilted sign, a dented bin, a door ajar, a board missing.

## Materials and textures

- **Blender-built assets:** plain palette materials (`pal.<name>`), roughness and metallic from the
  palette, no textures, no procedural shaders (they don't survive GLB export). UVs are box-projected
  at 1 m per unit, ready for a palette atlas later.
- **Meshy assets:** one texture, at most 2K, toned to the palette in Blender (`zchar.tone_texture`).
  Textures should look like flat colour areas: patterns (plaid, stripes) only at the scale of a hand,
  stains as a few big simple shapes. If a generated texture is busy, posterise or repaint it.
- **No emissive** except light sources (lamps, screens at night), and no transparency except glass.

## Characters

- **Modular: one base body, interchangeable wearables.** Every character is a base body in a plain
  fitted base layer on one shared rig, plus wearables: clothes (shirts, hoodies, jackets, trousers,
  shoes, gloves) and accessories (glasses, hats, scarves, bags). Each wearable is its own mesh, made
  against the base body's rest pose, so it fits anyone on that body: any survivor, and the zombies,
  who reuse the same body and wardrobe (a player who turns rises in what they wore). Soft wearables
  are skinned to the rig; rigid ones (glasses, hats) sit on a named socket (`eyes`, `head_top`,
  `neck`, `hand_r`, `hand_l`, `back`). A wearable hides the body regions it covers (head, torso,
  arms, hands, legs, feet) so nothing pokes through, and stands off the body like the real thing:
  glasses a centimetre in front of the face, not wrapped round it.
- **Build and proportions:** survivors 1.78 m, zombies 1.72 m stooped (asset-pipeline.md).
  Ordinary, grounded people: slim to average builds, realistic proportions with only a slightly
  large head and hands; not heroic, muscular, fat, cartoonish, voxel-blocky or doll-like. The
  survivor is someone going through something terrible: tired, worried, unsmiling.
- **Shading:** smooth within a facet, sharp across edges over 30°; about 1,800 triangles for a body,
  under 800 for a garment, under 100 for a small accessory (performance matters: every wearable
  adds to the body).
- **Faces:** a few planes; eyes and brows as dark shapes; no fine features. Zombies: sunken dark eyes,
  slack mouth.
- **Skin:** survivors in natural tones; zombies grey-green, toned from `brand_zombie_green` toward
  grey (around #8A9A7C), never bright.
- **Clothing:** everyday early-1990s small-town: flannel, work jackets, hoodies, jeans, scrubs,
  short-sleeved office shirts and ties, tracksuits, dresses, uniforms. One or two muted colours per
  garment, hanging close to the body (a fabric's thickness off it, never bulging).
- **Blood and grime:** a few muted dark-brown or dark-red stains, no wounds, gore or bones.
- **No protected emblems:** no red cross on a white field (use a plain stripe or a different mark for
  medical things), no real logos, brands or police insignia.
- **The player** reads at a glance by what they wear; other players differ by their wearables.

## Light and mood

Light is the first tool for the mood, and most of it is code (`daynight.rs`, `camera.rs`, the fog of
war in `fog.rs` and `world.wesl`). Numbers live there; this is what they're for.

- **Grade (every frame):** a filmic tone map; a warm, slightly desaturated grade (amber highlights,
  shadows a touch cool and lifted so dark areas stay readable as shapes); a soft vignette that draws
  the eye to the player and makes the edges of the world feel far away. Bloom only on real light
  sources (a flashlight, a candle, a lit window), never on sunlit surfaces.
- **Day:** a low, warm sun for most of the day (never overhead), long hard shadows, little fill light,
  so a building's shadow side is clearly darker. A faint haze. Late afternoon is the look to aim for,
  and since Linear DCM-59 the whole day leans that way: the sun is golden even at its highest (about
  47° up at noon, 32° by mid-afternoon) and deep amber toward dawn and dusk, the fill is a weak violet
  so shadows stay strong and cool against it, and the grade is warm with soft contrast and blacks a
  little lifted and faded (`daynight::grade_at`). What you can't see is no longer a grey hole by day:
  it keeps over half its colour, dimmed and warmed (`materials::unseen_tint`, `unseen_colour`), so the
  town reads as one sunlit place and your own view is the brightest part of it.
- **Film (DCM-59, `film.rs`, `film.wesl`):** one pass after the tone map: a little tilt-shift (the top
  and bottom of the screen go soft, at most 2.6 px of a 1080-high picture, the middle band stays
  sharp; the town reads as a small model) and fine grain (new 24 times a second, strongest in the
  mid-tones, none in black, about half strength at night). Keep both slight: they should be felt, not
  noticed.
- **Contact shadows (DCM-59):** a soft dark strip, 0.6 m wide, where every ground-floor wall meets the
  ground or a floor (`world_render::contact_shadows`), and every blade of grass is darker at its root.
  Together they stand in for ambient occlusion without a screen-space pass.
- **Dusk:** deep amber turning to grey-blue; shadows stretch across the street; the town goes quiet.
- **Night:** dark. Cool, very low moonlight outdoors is enough to read the edges of roads, roofs and a
  silhouette close by, no more. Unlit interiors are darker than outdoors. What you see is what your
  light reaches: a **flashlight** is a narrow, hard-edged cone along your facing that casts shadows
  (furniture and zombies throw long ones away from you); a **candle** is a small warm pool. Without a
  light you see a few metres, **but what is lit is seen**: the warm pool under a lit window, another
  survivor's beam across the street and whatever stands in them show from far off, with the dark
  between you and them. A light is company and a warning both. Zombies' senses are the sim's own (`ZombieTuning`); a light that draws
  them is a sim change of its own.
- **Inside by day:** windows are the light; the far side of a room is dimmer; a room with no window
  is dark.
- **Fog of war** is subtle by day: what the player can't see is a little dimmer and a touch faded,
  and the view cone ahead only slightly brighter than what's behind, with no ring round the player
  (the numbers are `assets/data/fog.ron`). At night it turns what's unseen nearly black. Every asset must still read as a silhouette dimmed, and buildings' outlines and ways in stay
  readable (Gate 4: check it dimmed and at night by flashlight).
- **Frame cost** is reported for every lighting change (offscreen 1920 × 1080 with 2,000 zombies at
  26 m and 60 m, `--frame-bench N` or `ZOMBYE_FRAME_BENCH`). Screen-space effects that need a depth
  prepass (screen-space ambient occlusion) wait until the cutaway and the window glass work with one.
- **Every effect is a setting** (`graphics.rs`): grain, tilt-shift, vignette, bloom, contact shadows
  and the ground's per-pixel detail each switch off by themselves (`--no-post`). They are gathered
  into presets (`--graphics low|medium|high|ultra|auto`, F8 in game): **High is the look this
  document describes** and the default; Medium and Low trade the subtler parts (tilt-shift first,
  then grain, ground detail, contact shadows, thinner grass, smaller shadow map) for frame rate;
  Ultra adds denser grass and a finer shadow map. A new effect joins that list and gets a place in
  each preset. Every change to the look reports its frame cost on a real GPU next to the pictures.
- Review renders in Blender use the Standard view transform (asset-pipeline.md), so the palette is
  what you see there; in game the grade warms and darkens it, which is why Gate 4 is in game.

## Decay and clutter

The world's generated parts and its props both carry the story. Targets for a default-zoom frame
(26 m) of an ordinary street, not a rule for every square metre:

- **Ground:** asphalt with cracks, a few heaved or sunken slabs and potholes, darker patches; weeds
  and grass in cracks and along kerbs; drifts of leaf litter in corners and gutters; loose paper,
  cans, bottles; a few dark stains. Lawns gone long and patchy, vacant lots and verges in tall grass
  with a few wildflowers (muted red, white, yellow) (#81).
- **Grass, flowers and litter, instanced (Linear DCM-59, `vegetation.rs`):** over the mesher's ground
  goes a layer of small shared meshes, one patch a tile, placed from the map alone
  (`landscape::scatter`), so the same town always grows the same grass. **Where:** long grass (to
  half a metre) where nobody mows: along walls, fences and hedges, verges, vacant lots, the forest's
  edge; shorter grass on lawns; thin, dry grass on dirt, under trees, beside paths and driveways and
  over the lawn's bare patches; none on a doorstep (the ground within about 3 m of an outside door is
  worn to earth), a driveway, a flower bed or under a porch. Wildflowers come in drifts a few metres
  across, red in some parts of town and white in others, thick in long grass and stray on lawns.
  Pavements get a weed in a crack now and then, a few loose sheets of paper and small stones; roads
  less, gutters more. **Colour:** each blade runs from a dark root to a light tip, and each patch is
  its own mix of `grass_blade` (fresh) and `grass_blade_dry` (straw), dry in broad drifts: the fields
  should read golden, not green. **Motion:** it sways in gusts that roll across it and bends away
  from the survivor's feet. **Not yet:** zombies don't part it, and it casts no shadows.
- **The ground itself** is broken up per pixel (`world.wesl` `ground_detail`): broad lighter and darker
  drifts, flat triangular facets a metre or so across (the reference's faceted earth, never smooth
  noise), and dry yellow patches on grass and dirt. Subtle on asphalt and concrete.
- **Things left behind:** two or three per street frame: a car left with a door open or rusting on
  flat tyres, a bike on its side, bins tipped over, a pram, suitcases, boxes, pallets, tyres, barrels;
  near the core, barricades: sandbags, boards, a police cordon (#61).
- **Buildings:** boarded windows here and there, a broken pane, a door ajar, gutters hanging, roofs
  with rows of shingles and the odd gap, brick on shops and offices, units and vents on flat roofs,
  porches with railings, faded signs (#59).
- **Inside:** clutter on floors and counters (papers, boxes, a fallen chair), drawers left open after
  a search, a few stains (#19, with the loot work).
- **Density never costs the crowd:** clutter is merged into chunk meshes or instanced, with a budget
  per chunk, and measured like any other rendering change. The instanced layer is drawn only round
  the camera (`vegetation::veg_radius`: just past the corners of the view, at most 60 m, shrinking to
  nothing over the last sixth so nothing pops), has a density setting (`--vegetation off|low|high|N`,
  `graphics.vegetation` in `settings.ron`) and its meshes stay under 100 triangles a patch.

## UI

- **One rough painted skin** (Linear DCM-61; `crates/client/src/theme.rs`, which every panel uses):
  as if laid on with a wide dry brush. The textures are made in code at start-up; none is a file,
  and none is drawn after another game's art.
- **Panels:** dark charcoal sheets (about #16161A at 96% opacity: slightly see-through), their sides
  ragged where the strokes end and thin out, top and bottom a rough line; brushed bone-white rules
  at low opacity. The small pieces of the HUD, tooltips and menus are strips of the same paint.
- **Titles** centred, in capitals; generous padding (nothing stands on a sheet's ragged edge).
- **Buttons:** plain, light (warm grey, dark ink on it), with a worn edge; the chosen one gold. A
  key to press is shown as a small button of the same kind.
- **Slots** (the hotbar): lighter charcoal with a fine scored hatch and a pale worn rim; the one in
  your hand has a gold edge.
- **Bars** (health, a pane's load, a skill's XP, the current action): one stroke of a dry brush
  over a paler one.
- **Status icons:** simple glyphs on a dark dab of paint, bottom left: white at the first level,
  then pale and strong yellow; only the worst level is red.
- **Text:** bone white for content, a dimmer warm grey for secondary text; one plain sans-serif
  family: Barlow for text and the HUD, Barlow Condensed SemiBold in capitals for titles, tabs and
  headers (SIL OFL 1.1, `assets/fonts/barlow/`; `theme::font`, `strong`, `head`). Monospace only on
  the developer's read-outs (the stats line, the debug stats window, the light preview).
- **Colour meanings:** red for blood, danger and health nearly gone; yellow for warnings; zombie
  green for sickness; cool blue for sleep; gold for level-ups, what's picked and good outcomes. No
  other hues.
- **Plain words** over icons until icons exist; icons later follow these same shapes and colours.

## Building assets in Blender over MCP

The [blender-assets skill](../.claude/skills/blender-assets/SKILL.md) has the commands. For the look:

1. **Start from the spec and a reference:** an approved asset of the same category if there is one,
   else the mood board image for that kind of thing.
2. **Block out** with `zasset.Mesh` primitives (`box`, `block`, `prism`, `blob`, `poly`) at real
   sizes. Check the silhouette from the four angles before any colour.
3. **Pick the colours first:** write down the 3–6 palette names, one dominant. Assign per part and per
   face (`faces={"top": ...}`) so tops and fronts carry the contrast.
4. **Add the telling details,** each at least 5 cm, and one-segment bevels where the light catches.
5. **Stay low-poly:** no subdivision; a modifier stack, if used, is bevel (1 segment) then weld, both
   applied before export. Flat shading everywhere on hard surfaces.
6. **Review sheet** from the game's four angles beside the scale figure and door frame; at Gate 3
   beside the reference and approved neighbours.
7. **Check in game** at 26 m and 12 m zoom, by day and at night (`ZOMBYE_EXTRA_MODELS`, `--render-to`).

## Prompts for generators (Meshy)

Prompts are our own words, describing our own designs. Never name another game, studio, artist or
asset vendor, and never use their images as input.

**Style prefix** (start every prompt with it):

> Stylised low-poly 3D video game art. Faceted, flat-shaded geometry with crisp hard edges and no
> smoothing, rendered in 3D with no outlines or ink lines; simple chunky shapes with a few telling
> details. Flat blocks of colour from a small, muted, slightly desaturated palette: no gradients, no
> photo textures, no fine patterns. Sparing accents of soft zombie green, signal red and bone white.
> Early-1990s small-town rural America, abandoned and decaying. Mood: lonely, tense, quietly
> frightening; never gory. No text, no user interface.

**Character reference** (then image-to-3D; meshy-assets skill):

> [prefix] Full-body character reference for a low-poly game model, single front view, T-pose, arms
> straight out, legs slightly apart, feet flat, plain empty background, even flat lighting, whole body
> in frame. Faceted low-poly person with stylised but human proportions (a slightly large head and
> hands), not blocky or voxel. Simple face of a few planes. Clothing as flat blocks of one or two muted
> colours. [Who: age, build, job, clothes, colours.] [Zombie: grey-green skin, sunken dark eyes, slack
> mouth, stooped; a few large muted dark stains, torn at a sleeve or knee; no wounds, no gore.]

**Prop or furniture reference** (for Gate 1; the model itself is built in Blender):

> [prefix] A single [thing], three-quarter view from above at 30 degrees, plain light grey background,
> even light. Real early-1990s size and shape: [size, materials, colours from the palette in words].
> One or two telling details: [details].

**Scene or mood image:**

> [prefix] Seen from high above at a 30 degree isometric angle with an orthographic camera. [Light:
> low warm late-afternoon sun with long shadows and haze / deep amber dusk / near-darkness lit only by
> a flashlight cone or a candle.] Subject: [scene].

Record every prompt, model and task id in the asset's `meta.yml` (art repo).

## Checklist for every review

- [ ] Reads from high up at default zoom, from all four angles, dimmed in fog, under the day grade,
      and at night by flashlight.
- [ ] Flat-shaded, faceted, no outlines, no smooth blobs on hard things.
- [ ] Three to six palette colours, one dominant; brand colours only as accents.
- [ ] Two or three telling details, none smaller than 5 cm; tops and fronts carry them.
- [ ] Early-1990s small town; no logos, readable text, protected emblems or gore.
- [ ] Sits beside the approved assets of its kind without standing out.
- [ ] Looks used or left, not new (rule 3), unless it's meant to be new.

## Follow-ups

The look asks for things the game doesn't do yet; each is its own issue on the board:
the light and grade (#83), decay and tall grass on the ground (#81), things left behind in the streets
(#61), the building kit's details (trim, porches, chimneys, siding lines, shopfronts and awnings,
boarded windows, #59) and the generated world's colours moved into the shared palette (#60).
The look and feel pass (Linear DCM-59: the golden day, the film pass, contact shadows, instanced
grass, flowers and litter, worn ground by doors) left for later: softer sun shadows (percentage-closer
soft shadows are behind an experimental Bevy feature), zombies parting the grass, vegetation
shadows, leaf litter and cans and bottles as litter kinds, and Blender-built clutter props.
