# Art direction

How Zombye looks, so every asset (built in Blender, started in Meshy, or generated by the game's
code) belongs to the same world. [asset-pipeline.md](asset-pipeline.md) is the process and the
sizes; this is the look. Colours live in one place, [`tools/blender/palette.py`](../tools/blender/palette.py);
this doc says how to use them.

## The look in one sentence

**A quiet early-1990s American small town after the end, in faceted low-poly: flat blocks of muted
colour, simple chunky shapes with a few telling details, read from high above; a little spooky and a
little playful, never gory.**

Five rules follow from it:

1. **Faceted, flat-shaded, hard edges.** Every face reads as a plane of one colour. No smooth
   shading on hard-surface things, no outlines or ink lines, no painted shading baked into colours.
2. **Few colours per asset.** Three to six palette colours for a prop or a house, one of them
   dominant. Variety comes from picking a different set per building or outfit, not from more colours
   on one thing.
3. **Simple shapes, a few telling details.** The silhouette says what it is; then two or three
   details make it ours: window trim and a porch on a house, a handle and door line on a fridge,
   burners on a stove, a flag on a mailbox. Nothing that matters is smaller than about 5 cm (one
   pixel at default zoom is ~3.6 cm).
4. **Muted world, sparing accents.** The town is desaturated and a little dusty. The brand colours
   (zombie green, red, bone white, near-black) are accents: a sign, a hydrant, a toy, a zombie's
   skin, the UI. If the brand colours dominate a frame, there's too much of them.
5. **Playful-spooky from shape, not colour or gore.** Stooped, shambling, slightly chunky zombies;
   lopsided props, a tipped-over bin, a door left open. Blood is a few muted dark stains, never
   wounds, organs or bones.

## Colour

### The palette

`palette.py` is the only place colours are defined (validation refuses any other). Groups:

| Group | Entries | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Brand accents | `brand_zombie_green` #8DB06B, `brand_red` #D7262B, `brand_bone_white` #F2E6C9, `brand_near_black` #141414 | signs, hydrants, toys, stripes; zombie skin is toned from the green; UI |
| Wood | pine, oak, walnut, raw | furniture, floors, fences |
| Kitchen and bathroom | laminate almond, cabinet oak, enamel white and almond, porcelain, water | appliances, fittings |
| Metal and plastic | steel, chrome, iron black, plastic black/grey/beige/red/blue, screen, rubber, paint green, cardboard, paper | trims, handles, small props |
| Fabric | mauve, teal, rust, navy, beige, cream sheets, white pillows, blanket blue | upholstery, beds, clothing |

**Adding a colour:** only when no entry is close enough (within about 10% in each channel) and the
asset needs it to read; add it to `palette.py` in its group with a comment saying what it's for. Keep
new colours in the same family: saturation under about 45%, nothing pure, nothing neon. Brand accents
stay four.

### The generated world

Walls, roofs, floors, ground and trees are made by the client's code (`meshgen.rs`, `world_render.rs`)
and follow the same rules. Their colours today are tuned by eye in code; they move into the shared
palette with the building kit work (see Follow-ups).

| Surface | Look |
|---|---|
| House siding | one muted colour per house from a small set: cream, almond, butter, dusty blue, sage, grey, tan, oxblood |
| Trim (window and door frames, corners, fascia) | off-white on most houses, dark on pale ones |
| Roofs | dark slate, brown, rust red, moss, slate blue; a shade darker on the far slope |
| Shops and offices | brick red and tan, flat roofs with a parapet, awnings in one muted colour and bone white |
| Interiors | a wall colour per room type (kitchen cream, bathroom pale blue, bedroom dusty rose, living warm beige); plank wood, carpet, checker tile |
| Ground | olive-grey grass in patches, grey asphalt with faded yellow lines, pale concrete sidewalks, brown dirt |
| Trees | dark green cones (pines) and lumpy round crowns (broadleaf), a few greens per tree type, brown trunks |

## Shape and detail

| Thing | Shapes | Telling details | Triangles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small prop, ground item | one or two primitives | one: a lid, a label band, a handle | ≤ 300 |
| Furniture | boxes and blocks with small bevels on the parts you see | 2–3: drawer and door lines, handles, cushions, legs | 400–1,000 |
| Street furniture (mailbox, hydrant, bin, lamp, sign) | posts and boxes, a prism or two | 1–2: a flag, caps, a hood | ≤ 500 |
| Vehicle | a boxy early-90s body in two or three blocks | wheels, bumpers, lights, windows as dark panels | ≤ 3,000 |
| Character | one faceted body, chunky hands and feet | face as a few planes and colours, collar, belt, shoes | ~3,000 |

- **Bevels** only where they catch the light from above (table tops, cushions, counter fronts), one
  segment, then apply. Small hard bevels are what make low-poly look made rather than unfinished.
- **Proportions** may be pushed a little for readability: handles a bit big, shapes a bit chunky,
  never cartoon-squashed.
- **Facing up matters most.** The camera sits 30° above the horizon, so tops (beds, tables, counters,
  car roofs) are what the player sees. Put the telling details on top and on the front.
- **Wear** is shape, not texture: a tilted sign, a dented bin, a door ajar, a board missing.

## Materials and textures

- **Blender-built assets:** plain palette materials (`pal.<name>`), roughness and metallic from the
  palette, no textures, no procedural shaders (they don't survive GLB export). UVs are box-projected
  at 1 m per unit, ready for a palette atlas later.
- **Meshy assets:** one texture, at most 2K, toned to the palette in Blender (`zchar.tone_texture`).
  Textures should look like flat colour areas: patterns (plaid, stripes) only at the scale of a hand,
  stains as a few big simple shapes. If a generated texture is busy, posterise or repaint it.
- **No emissive** except light sources (lamps, screens at night), and no transparency except glass.

## Characters

- **Proportions:** survivors 1.78 m, zombies 1.72 m stooped (asset-pipeline.md). Stylised but
  human: a slightly large head and hands, a sturdy torso, never voxel-blocky or doll-like.
- **Faces:** a few planes; eyes and brows as dark shapes; no fine features. Zombies: sunken dark eyes,
  slack mouth.
- **Skin:** survivors in natural tones; zombies grey-green, toned from `brand_zombie_green` toward
  grey (around #8A9A7C), never bright.
- **Clothing:** everyday early-1990s small-town: flannel, work jackets, jeans, scrubs, short-sleeved
  office shirts and ties, tracksuits, dresses, uniforms. One or two muted colours per garment; outfits
  are variants on one base mesh per body type.
- **Blood and grime:** a few muted dark-brown or dark-red stains, no wounds, gore or bones.
- **No protected emblems:** no red cross on a white field (use a plain stripe or a different mark for
  medical things), no real logos, brands or police insignia.
- **The player** reads at a glance: mustard jacket (`JACKET_ME`); other players teal.

## Light and time of day

- **Day:** warm sun from high up, soft long shadows, a pale blue sky fill. Colours as in the palette.
- **Dusk:** warmer, longer shadows.
- **Night:** cool blue moonlight, low; warm practical lights (lamps, windows, the player's light)
  are the only warm pools. Dark enough that sneaking matters, light enough to read silhouettes.
- **Fog of war** darkens and desaturates what the player can't see. Every asset must still read
  as a silhouette there: check it dimmed (Gate 4).
- Review renders use the Standard view transform (asset-pipeline.md), so the palette is what you see.

## UI

- **Panels:** near-black (#141414 at 80–88% opacity), square corners, thin bone-white rules at low
  opacity.
- **Text:** bone white for content, a dimmer warm grey for secondary text; one plain sans-serif.
- **Colour meanings:** red for health, blood and danger; amber for endurance and warnings; zombie
  green for sickness; cool blue for sleep; gold for level-ups and good outcomes. No other hues.
- **Plain words** over icons until icons exist; icons later follow these same shapes and colours.

## Building assets in Blender over MCP

The [blender-assets skill](../.claude/skills/blender-assets/SKILL.md) has the commands. For the look:

1. **Start from the spec and a reference:** an approved asset of the same category if there is one,
   else the mood board image for that kind of thing.
2. **Block out** with `zasset.Mesh` primitives (`box`, `block`, `prism`, `blob`, `poly`) at real
   sizes. Check the silhouette from the four angles before any colour.
3. **Pick the colours first:** write down the 3–6 palette names, one dominant. Assign per part and per
   face (`faces={"top": ...}`) so tops and fronts carry the contrast.
4. **Add the telling details,** each at least 5 cm, and one-segment bevels where the light catches.
5. **Stay low-poly:** no subdivision; a modifier stack, if used, is bevel (1 segment) then weld, both
   applied before export. Flat shading everywhere on hard surfaces.
6. **Review sheet** from the game's four angles beside the scale figure and door frame; at Gate 3
   beside the reference and approved neighbours.
7. **Check in game** at 26 m and 12 m zoom, by day and at night (`ZOMBYE_EXTRA_MODELS`, `--render-to`).

## Prompts for generators (Meshy)

Prompts are our own words, describing our own designs. Never name another game, studio, artist or
asset vendor, and never use their images as input.

**Style prefix** (start every prompt with it):

> Stylised low-poly 3D video game art. Faceted, flat-shaded geometry with crisp hard edges and no
> smoothing, rendered in 3D with no outlines or ink lines; simple chunky shapes with a few telling
> details. Flat blocks of colour from a small, muted, slightly desaturated palette: no gradients, no
> photo textures, no fine patterns. Sparing accents of soft zombie green, signal red and bone white.
> Early-1990s small-town rural America. Mood: quiet, eerie, a little playful; never gory. No text, no
> user interface.

**Character reference** (then image-to-3D; meshy-assets skill):

> [prefix] Full-body character reference for a low-poly game model, single front view, T-pose, arms
> straight out, legs slightly apart, feet flat, plain empty background, even flat lighting, whole body
> in frame. Faceted low-poly person with stylised but human proportions (a slightly large head and
> hands), not blocky or voxel. Simple face of a few planes. Clothing as flat blocks of one or two muted
> colours. [Who: age, build, job, clothes, colours.] [Zombie: grey-green skin, sunken dark eyes, slack
> mouth, stooped; a few large muted dark stains, torn at a sleeve or knee; no wounds, no gore.]

**Prop or furniture reference** (for Gate 1; the model itself is built in Blender):

> [prefix] A single [thing], three-quarter view from above at 30 degrees, plain light grey background,
> even light. Real early-1990s size and shape: [size, materials, colours from the palette in words].
> One or two telling details: [details].

**Scene or mood image:**

> [prefix] Seen from high above at a 30 degree isometric angle with an orthographic camera. [Light:
> soft daylight / dusk / cool moonlight with warm lamp pools.] Subject: [scene].

Record every prompt, model and task id in the asset's `meta.yml` (art repo).

## Checklist for every review

- [ ] Reads from high up at default zoom, from all four angles, and dimmed in fog.
- [ ] Flat-shaded, faceted, no outlines, no smooth blobs on hard things.
- [ ] Three to six palette colours, one dominant; brand colours only as accents.
- [ ] Two or three telling details, none smaller than 5 cm; tops and fronts carry them.
- [ ] Early-1990s small town; no logos, readable text, protected emblems or gore.
- [ ] Sits beside the approved assets of its kind without standing out.

## Follow-ups

The look asks for things the game doesn't do yet; each is its own issue on the board:
the building kit's details (trim, porches, chimneys, siding lines, shopfronts and awnings, #59) and the
generated world's colours moved into the shared palette (#60).
